# **CRIMINAL PROCEDURE**

## **An Intro to Criminal Procedure**

### **An Intro to Criminal Procedure** 

### 

**Criminal Procedure**  
Comes from statutes and Constitution (mostly 4th, 5th, and 6th Amendments)

**Exclusionary Rule:** In most cases, if the government violates criminal procedure rules in the Constitution, it won’t be able to benefit from the violation—specifically, the government is prohibited from using any evidence it obtained as a result of its violation

**4th Amendment**  
People have right to be free from unreasonable searches and seizures, and warrants must be based on probable cause

* Only applies to government conduct (public officials or those acting on behalf of public officials), not private conduct  
* The term “warrants” means both arrest warrants and search warrants  
* There are exceptions to the warrant requirement, namely circumstances in which the government doesn’t need a warrant

**5th Amendment**

* Protects people against government-compelled self-incrimination  
* Protects people against double jeopardy, ensuring person won’t be prosecuted twice for same offense

**6th Amendment**

* Right to counsel in all critical stages of criminal prosecutions  
* Confrontation Clause gives criminal defendants the opportunity to question witnesses who have testified against them, usually on cross examination  
* Speedy Trial guarantees that the government may not charge a person with a crime and make them wait indefinitely for trial

**14th Amendment**

* Due Process Clause ensures that no state shall make or enforce any laws that deprive people of life, liberty, or property without due process of law  
* Under the doctrine of selective incorporation, some constitutional amendments are deemed so important that they are binding on the states, such as  
  * The 4th Amendment freedom from unreasonable searches and seizures  
  * The 5th Amendment privilege against self-incrimination  
  * The 5th Amendment prohibition against double jeopardy  
  * The 6th Amendment right to speedy trial  
  * The 6th Amendment right to confrontation  
  * The 6th Amendment right to counsel in criminal prosecutions  
* These are the minimum protections that states must provide; they can provide more

## **Watch Criminal Procedure Foundations Episode: 4th Amendment: Seizures of the Person**

### **4th Amendment –**

### **Seizures of the Person**

### 

**4th Amendment**  
People have a right to be secure in their persons against unreasonable searches and seizures

**A seizure** occurs when the government exercises control over a person or thing

* A seizure has occurred if, under the totality of the circumstances, the average person would not feel free to decline an officer’s request or terminate the encounter altogether  
* A seizure requires either a physical application of force by a police officer, or a show of police force and a person’s submission to that show of force

**Unreasonable**

* Investigatory Detention  
  * Considered reasonable if, under the totality of the circumstances, police officer has reasonable suspicion of criminal activity based on articulable facts  
    * Articulable Fact: Something you can explain with words  
  * Officer may detain person long enough to confirm or dispel the suspicion  
* Misdemeanors  
  * Unreasonable to arrest person for a misdemeanor without an arrest warrant unless misdemeanor was committed in officer’s presence  
* Felonies  
  * Reasonable to arrest person for felony without a warrant if officer has probable cause to believe a felony was committed and that the person in front of them committed it  
    * Probable Cause: Knowledge of reasonably trustworthy facts and circumstances that would lead a reasonable person to believe that suspect has committed a crime for which arrest is authorized

## **Evidentiary Search and Seizure**

### **Evidentiary Search and Seizure**

### 

   
**4th Amendment Searches**  
A search occurs when there is governmental intrusion into an area where a person has a reasonable expectation of privacy

**Expectation of Privacy**  
If you hold something out to the public, you don’t have an expectation of privacy in that thing

* No expectation of privacy in smell of items, records held by a third party, or yard viewable from a place police officer is allowed to be

**Standing**  
To challenge search or seizure, person must have standing to challenge governmental conduct: must be a violation of complaining person’s own expectation of privacy

**Warrants**  
A warrantless search is presumptively unreasonable unless an exception to the warrant requirement applies

* To be valid, search warrants must  
  * Be issued by a neutral and detached magistrate  
  * Be based on probable cause; and  
  * Describe with particularity the place to be searched and the items to be seized  
* **Remedies for Violating Warrant Requirement**  
  * Exclusionary Rule  
    * Evidence directly or derivatively obtained in violation of 4th Amendment is generally not admissible against the defendant  
    * If officers reasonably and in good faith rely on a warrant later determined to be invalid, evidence won’t be excluded  
  * Fruit of the Poisonous Tree

    * Most evidence derived from unconstitutionally obtained evidence is inadmissible  
* **How Warrants are Executed**

  * Must be executed by the police, unaccompanied by third parties, and without unreasonable delay  
  * Officers must knock and announce their presence and wait a reasonable amount of time and be refused entry before using force  
    * No-Knock Warrants  
      * Allowed if knocking would be dangerous, futile, or potentially lead to destruction of evidence  
      * Exclusionary rule doesn’t apply

What are the three requirements of a valid search warrant?  

Correct  
To be valid, a warrant must:

1. Be issued by a neutral and detached magistrate,  
2. Be based on probable cause established from facts submitted to the magistrate by a government agent upon oath or affirmation, and  
3. Particularly describe the place to be searched and the items to be seized.

A valid warrant need NOT be accompanied by a bond for damages.

Before executing a search warrant at a private residence, the police must \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ knock and announce their presence. 

Correct

When executing a warrant, the police do not need to always knock and announce their presence before entering a home.

Ordinarily, the police must knock and announce their authority and purpose and await admittance for a reasonable time or be refused admittance before using force to enter. However, no announcement needs to be made if the police reasonably suspect that knocking and announcing would be dangerous or futile or inhibit the investigation.

## **4th Amendment: Exceptions to the Warrant Requirement Part 1**

### **4th Amendment –**

### **Exceptions to the Warrant Requirement (Part 1\)**

### 

   
In general, police need a warrant to conduct a search or make a seizure

Search or Seizure \+ No Warrant \+ No Exceptions \= Inadmissible at Trial

**Exceptions to Warrant Requirement**

**Search Incident to Lawful Arrest**  
Police are allowed to conduct warrantless search after they make an arrest as long as arrest was lawful

* Lawful arrest \= Made based on probable cause  
* May search anywhere where person can reach  
* Valid only if done contemporaneously with arrest  
* Automobile arrests  
  * When a person who was in automobile is arrested, police may search interior of car if  
    * Arrestee is unsecured and still may gain access to interior of vehicle; or  
    * Police reasonably believe evidence of offense for which the person was arrested may be found inside the vehicle

**Automobile Exception**  
If police have probable cause to believe a car contains evidence of a crime, then they can search car without a warrant

* Police can search any area of car that might contain evidence for which they have probable cause to search  
* Searches aren’t limited to driver’s belongings, can extend to items belonging to passengers  
* Doesn’t have to be contemporaneous with stop of vehicle

**Plain View**  
Police may make warrantless seizure when

* They are legitimately on premises  
* They see an item in plain view  
* It’s readily apparent that item is contraband or evidence, fruits, or instrumentalities of a crime

**Consent**  
Police may conduct warrantless search if, under the totality of the circumstances, they have voluntary consent to do so

* No coercion  
* Any person who has apparent authority may consent  
* Any evidence discovered may be used against actual owner or anyone else occupying property  
* If no actual authority to consent, search still valid if reasonable for officer to believe there was authority to consent  
* Scope of consent ➔ Only search where consent was given

A driver was stopped for speeding. Because of the speed at which she was traveling and because the driver possessed an out-of-state driver’s license, the officer decided to place the driver under arrest and take her to the station house. Under state law, such an arrest is valid. After placing the driver in his patrol car, the officer searched the passenger area of the automobile. Under the front seat, he found a small package containing what he immediately determined to be cocaine. Was this a proper search?

Correct  
The correct answer is (C). The automobile exception to the warrant requirement permits a stopped vehicle to be searched in the absence of a warrant if the police have probable cause to believe that the vehicle contains seizeable evidence. Here, there were no facts suggesting that the officer had established probable cause that evidence of a crime could be found inside the car.

(A) is incorrect because it is contrary to the facts. Nothing in the facts suggests the police had probable cause to believe the driver was involved in any criminal activity other than speeding, so the officer did not have probable cause to believe the car contained items that were legitimate to seize.

(B) is incorrect.   
Under the search incident to a lawful arrest exception, if the police lawfully arrest the occupant of a vehicle, they can search the vehicle only if:

1. The arrestee is unsecured and still may gain access to the interior of the vehicle; or  
2. They reasonably believe that evidence of the offense for which the person was arrested may be found inside the vehicle.

   
Here, the driver was secured and there was no way the officer could reasonably believe that they were going to find evidence of the offense for which the driver was arrested, speeding, inside the car.  

**Under the plain view exception to the warrant requirement, which of the following is not required?**     
Correct  
For the warrantless seizure of an item under the plain view exception, it is not required that the item be inadvertently discovered.  
   
To make a warrantless seizure:

1. The police must be legitimately on the premises where the item is found;  
2. The item must be in plain view; and  
3. It must be readily apparent (that is, the police must have probable cause to believe without manipulation) that the item is contraband or evidence, a fruit, or an instrumentality of a crime.

## **Exceptions to the Warrant Requirement Part 2**

### **4th Amendment –**

### **Exceptions to the Warrant Requirement (Part 2\)**

### 

   
**Exceptions to Warrant Requirement**

**Stop and Frisk**  
Police may stop a person without a warrant or probable cause if officer has an articulable and reasonable suspicion of criminal activity

* Investigatory detention or *Terry* stop  
* Officer may sometimes also conduct limited search of person without a warrant when officer reasonably believes that person may be presently armed and dangerous  
  * Generally limited to pat down of outer clothing  
  * Plain feel, no manipulation

**Hot Pursuit**  
Pursuing a fleeing felon

* Can extend to wherever is reasonably necessary to prevent suspect from resisting or escaping  
* Not for misdemeanors unless there’s a law enforcement emergency  
  * Necessary to prevent imminent harm  
  * Destruction of evidence  
  * Suspect’s escape

**Exigent Circumstances**  
Prevent destruction of evidence

* Exigent circumstances can also exist if police believe someone on premises is in imminent danger

**Evanescent Evidence**  
If evidence will likely disappear before police are able to get a warrant

**Emergency Aid**  
Emergency that would threaten health or safety if police do not immediately act

**Community Caretaker**  
Police may make warrantless searches if they come across criminal activity while they are performing community caretaker duties (those not related to investigation of a crime)

* Not permitted to make warrantless entry into a home

**Other Exceptions**

* Government inspectors may conduct administrative inspections pursuant to a warrant if they demonstrate that the inspection is undertaken as part of a general and neutral enforcement plan  
  * This doesn't require a showing that a violation has already occurred  
* Warrants not required for police inventory searches or impounded cars  
* School officials may search students and their lockers and backpacks. Reasonable if  
  * Offers a moderate chance of finding evidence of wrongdoing  
  * Measures used to perform search are reasonably related to the objective of the search; and  
  * Search not excessively intrusive when considering age and sex of student and nature of the infraction  
* Students in extracurricular activities can be required to submit to random urinalysis drug testing monitored by an adult of the same sex

**Parolees**  
Have diminished expectation of privacy because they are out on parole

**Government Employees**  
Desks and file cabinets can be searched without a warrant (within reason)

**Warrantless Drug Testing**  
Government can require drug testing without probable cause or individualized suspicion when justified by a special need beyond the general interest of law enforcement

**Border Searches**  
Searches at border don’t require warrant, probable cause, or reasonable suspicion

Incorrect

Under the exigent circumstances exception, the police can enter a home without a warrant if there is imminent danger. Here, the officers were told that gunshots and shouting came from the home. This was an emergency and imminent danger that needed to be acted upon, so the warrantless entry was permissible under both the emergency aid and exigent circumstances exception.

(A) is incorrect because it is not the best answer. While the search was permissible under the emergency aid exception, as previously explained the warrantless entry was valid under both the emergency aid and exigent circumstances exceptions. Therefore, an answer choice of only emergency aid is not the best answer.

(B) is incorrect because the community caretaker exception does not permit warrantless entry into a home.

(C) is incorrect. Under the evanescent evidence exception to the warrant requirement, the police may make a warrantless search or seizure if the evidence will likely disappear before the police are able to obtain a warrant. Here, there were no facts to suggest that evidence would disappear if they would have waited for a warrant.

Under which of the following circumstances may an officer enter a home without a warrant?   
 Under the hot pursuit exception, the police may enter a home without a warrant to pursue someone suspected of a felony.   
Under the exigent circumstances exception, the police are permitted to enter a home without a warrant to prevent the destruction of evidence.

## **Confessions: 14th and 6th Amendments**

### **Confessions –**

### **14th and 6th Amendments**

### 

   
**14th Amendment**  
Due process requires confessions to be voluntary to be admissible

* Depends on totality of circumstances  
* Courts look at suspect’s age, education, mental and physical condition, and the setting, duration, and manner of interrogation  
* Coercion can be physical or mental  
* If suspect makes statement because of mental condition that impacts their free will, statement isn’t automatically considered involuntary  
* A conviction will not be overturned solely because a coerced confession was admitted, if there was other overwhelming evidence of guilt

**6th Amendment**

* Right to assistance of counsel when D is questioned **after** charges have been filed  
* Prosecution starts when formal charges have been filed and includes  
  * Post-indictment interrogations  
  * Preliminary hearings to determine probable cause to prosecute  
  * Arraignments  
  * Post-charge lineups  
  * Guilty pleas and sentencing  
  * Felony trials  
* No 6th Amendment right to counsel at  
  * Blood sampling  
  * Pre-charge or investigative lineups  
  * Photo identifications  
  * Preliminary hearings to determine probable cause to detain  
  * Post-conviction proceedings  
* Right is violated when police elicit an incriminating statement from D without first obtaining a waiver (must be knowing and voluntary)  
* Offense specific, so invoking right to counsel on one charge does not apply to all charges  
* Statements obtained in violation aren’t admissible in prosecution’s case in chief, but can be used to impeach D’s testimony  
* Subject to the **harmless error rule**, the remedy for using a statement obtained during a non-trial proceeding that violates the defendant’s right to counsel is reversal of the conviction

In which of the following situations does a suspect have a Sixth Amendment right to counsel?   
Correct A suspect has a Sixth Amendment right to counsel during all critical stages after formal proceedings have begun. Post-charge questioning is a critical stage that takes place after formal proceedings have begun. Thus, a defendant has a Sixth Amendment right to counsel at this stage. (A) is incorrect because there is no Sixth Amendment right to counsel before charges are filed. (C) is incorrect for the same reason that (A) is incorrect—there is no Sixth Amendment right to counsel before charges are filed. (D) is incorrect because there is no right to counsel at photo identifications whether they take place before or after charges are filed.

Correct The Fourteenth Amendment requires that confessions be voluntary in order to be admissible at trial. A confession will be rendered involuntary if it is the result of official compulsion. (A) is incorrect, as an interrogation is not necessarily official compulsion. Thus, an interrogation alone does not necessarily render a confession involuntary. (B) is incorrect because a confession will be considered involuntary only if there is police compulsion. If a defendant spontaneously makes a statement, there is no official compulsion.

## **Confessions: Miranda**

### **Confessions: *Miranda*** 

### 

   
**5th Amendment**  
Cannot be compelled to give self-incriminating testimony

***Miranda*** **Warnings**  
**When Required**

* Police must tell someone  
  * You have a right to remain silent  
  * You have a right to an attorney  
  * If you cannot afford an attorney, one will be provided to you at no charge  
  * Anything you say can be used against you in court  
* Warnings must occur when person is subject to custodial interrogation by someone known to be a police officer  
  * Custodial  
    * Would a reasonable person under the circumstances feel free to terminate the interrogation and leave?  
    * Does the environment present the same inherently coercive pressures as stationhouse questioning?  
    * Undercover officer \= No *Miranda* warnings needed  
  * Interrogation  
    * Any words or actions on the part of the police that they should know are reasonably likely to elicit an incriminating response from the suspect  
    * Not routine booking questions

   
If police fail to give *Miranda* warnings when they should have, the evidence generally can’t be used against the defendant

Suspect’s options after receiving *Miranda* warnings

* Make a knowing and voluntary waiver of *Miranda* rights  
* Remain silent  
  * Must say they want to remain silent in explicit, unambiguous, and unequivocal manner  
* Invoke 5th Amendment right to counsel in unambiguous and specific manner  
  * All questioning must stop until attorney is present or detainee initiates  
  * Lasts as long as detainee is in custody \+ 14 days

For purposes of Miranda, a person is in custody if:  Correct

A person is in custody if the relevant environment presents coercive pressures that are similar to that of stationhouse questioning and a reasonable person would feel that they were not free to leave and terminate the encounter.

(A) is incorrect because whether a person is in custody is based on an objective standard, not the person’s subjective belief.

(B) on its own is incorrect because the environment alone is not enough to render a person in custody.

(C) on its own is incorrect because a determination that a reasonable person would not have felt free to leave and terminate the encounter is not enough to render a person in custody.

Incorrect
